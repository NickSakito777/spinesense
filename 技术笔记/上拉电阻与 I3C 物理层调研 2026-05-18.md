# Pull-up resistors and the I3C physical layer: investigation (2026-05-18)

> Historical research note, translated in September 2026. Claims, estimates and design decisions below retain their status as recorded on 2026-05-18; they have not been silently corrected. For the current tested collection workflow, see the [Tom handoff](../docs/tom-handoff/README.md).

> Three independent agents fetched official datasheets, the MIPI specification and STM32 HAL source code in parallel. The aim was to settle three questions together: why SDA/SCL need pull-ups, whether the Nucleo and commercial sensor boards actually include them, and whether the STM32U385 provides an internal alternative.

## One-sentence conclusion

The I²C / I3C physical protocol layer **requires** pull-ups on SDA/SCL. Neither the Nucleo-U385RG-Q nor the STEVAL-MKI248KA kit **provides external pull-ups**, but the STM32U385 MCU itself **integrates dedicated I3C pull-ups** (PWR_I3CPUCR1/2 registers, the approach officially recommended in AN5879), which can be enabled in firmware. The SpineSense custom expansion-board specification nevertheless adopts **on-board SDA/SCL 0402 SMD pull-up resistors** (β=g) as the main approach, with internal pull-ups enabled as redundancy.

---

## 1. Current hardware

### 1.1 NUCLEO-U385RG-Q (MB1841E)

**Conclusion:** the board's Arduino D14/D15 (SDA/SCL) lines **have no integrated pull-up resistors**.

**Evidence:**

- UM3062 Table 16 (MB1841E variant, page 34): D14/D15 are routed only through SB42–SB45 to PB6/PB7 (I²C1) or PB13/PB14 (I3C2); there is no pull-up entry.
- UM3062 Table 19: SB9/SB10/SB11 are unrelated to SDA/SCL pull-ups.
- UM3062 revision table E01: “To support I²C/I3C compatible (ARDUINO D14/D15)” — adds signal routing only, not pull-ups.
- ST wiki AN5879 + ST Community consensus: Nucleo boards do not integrate I²C pull-ups.

### 1.2 STEVAL-MKI248KA kit

**Conclusion:** the kit itself (square IMU board + DIL24 adapter) **contains no SCL/SDA pull-up resistors at all**. Confirmed with 100% confidence by directly reading the schematics.

**Evidence:**

- **STEVAL-MKIGIBV5 (DIL24 adapter):** R1 = 0Ω (in series with OCS, NOT MOUNT), R2 = 0Ω (in series with INT2), C1/C2 = 330pF (VDDIO/VDD filtering). SDA/SCL are direct wires from J1 to JP2 pins 20/21, with no components.
- **STEVAL-MKI248AA (square IMU board):** U1 = ISM6HG256X, C1 = 100nF, C2 = 2.2µF, C3 = 100nF, and three headers. **There is no R designator anywhere on the board.**
- Data brief wording (page 1): “comes ready-to-use with the required **decoupling capacitors** on the VDD power supply line” — promises decoupling capacitors only, not pull-ups.

## 2. Why the protocol requires them

### 2.1 The I²C requirement

Original wording from NXP UM10204 §3.1.1:

> “Both SDA and SCL are bidirectional lines, connected to a positive supply voltage via a current-source or pull-up resistor.”

I²C is an open-drain bus: both slave and master can only pull a line low. **Without pull-ups, the lines physically cannot return High**, and the entire bus locks up.

### 2.2 The I3C requirement

I3C has two operating modes, **both of which require pull-ups**:

- **Open Drain mode** (Start condition / Address Header / ACK / SETDASA / ENTDAA / Hot-Join / IBI arbitration): like I²C, it needs OD pull-ups. The MIPI I3C FAQ states that the Controller must *“Manage its Pull-Up structures, including the Open Drain class Pull-Up and High-Keeper Pull-Up for both SDA and SCL”*.
- **Push-Pull SDR data phase:** SCL is always push-pull (the fundamental reason I3C does not support clock stretching); SDA is high-Z during the Read T-bit gap and requires a **High-Keeper (weak pull-up)** to maintain the level.
- **Bus Idle state** (MIPI I3C v1.x §5.1.2) + Hot-Join / IBI triggering: SDA + SCL must both be High → pull-ups are required.

Typical I3C OD pull-ups are tens of kΩ, weaker than I²C's 1–10 kΩ, because push-pull drive provides the edges during high-speed phases.

---

## 3. Physical consequences of missing pull-up resistors, in sequence

1. After power-up, SDA/SCL are high-Z, so **the bus cannot enter Idle**.
2. A Start condition requires SDA to fall while SCL is High. SCL cannot rise, so **Start is physically impossible**.
3. After release of the SETDASA ACK bit, the line needs to be pulled High. It floats, so **the Controller interprets a NACK**.
4. SDA is high-Z during the Read T-bit gap: **the level collapses and read data become misaligned**.
5. Hot-Join / IBI triggering depends on Bus Idle, so **events never trigger**.
6. Wired-AND arbitration fails: **multi-device SETDASA + GPIO TA0 switching fails collectively at the ACK step**.

## 4. STM32U385's internal dedicated I3C pull-ups (**key new finding**)

**Conclusion:** the STM32U385 provides **dedicated I3C pull-ups** in the PWR block (not ordinary 40 kΩ weak GPIO pull-ups). AN5879 officially recommends internal pull-ups instead of external resistors.

### 4.1 Registers

| Register | Location | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `PWR_I3CPUCR1` | PWR block offset 0xB0 | Pull-up enable bits for one group of candidate I3C pins |
| `PWR_I3CPUCR2` | PWR block offset 0xB4 | The other group |
| `I3C_CFGR.HKSDAEN` | I3C block bit 5 | High-Keeper on SDA; auxiliary level keeper during the Read T-bit gap |

**Candidate pins (PWR_I3CPUCR1):** PB6 / PB7 (I3C1_SCL/SDA on Arduino D15/D14), and PA1 / PA6 / PA7 / PB2 / PB8 / PB9 / PB10 / PB12–14 / PC0 / PC1 / PD12 / PD13 / PG7 / PG8 / PG13 / PG14 / PH3, among others.

### 4.2 HAL API

```c
HAL_PWREx_EnablePullUpPullDownConfig();     // Unlock latch writes first
HAL_PWREx_EnableI3CPullUp(PWR_I3CPU_PB13);  // SCL: enable the I3C pull-up on PB13
HAL_PWREx_EnableI3CPullUp(PWR_I3CPU_PB14);  // SDA: enable the I3C pull-up on PB14
```

Both the I3C peripheral configuration page and the PWR page in CubeMX expose these settings.

### 4.3 Resistance

- Fixed resistance, estimated to be on the order of **1.5–2.2 kΩ**.
- These are **strong, dedicated I3C pull-ups**, not weak keepers.
- The “I3C characteristics” table in DS14598 datasheet Section 7 gives the exact typical value (Fox was advised to check after obtaining the Windows machine + Nucleo on 5-20).

### 4.4 Explicit ST recommendation

- The **AN5879** CubeMX configuration screenshot explicitly says “no pull is needed”: with internal I3C pull-ups, external ones are unnecessary.
- The ST wiki “Getting started with I3C” also states that the STM32H5/U3/N6 I3C IP integrates dedicated pull-ups.

### 4.5 Rise-time estimate: 5 IMUs + 60 cm bus

Using `t_rise = 0.8473 × R × C`, with estimated bus capacitance of 100–145 pF:

| RPU | Bus C | t_rise | I3C OD specification limit ~150 ns |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 kΩ (estimated internal) | 100 pF | 170 ns | Slightly over |
| 1.5 kΩ | 100 pF | 127 ns | Pass |
| 2 kΩ | 145 pF | 246 ns | Over |
| 50 kΩ (only the weak HKSDAEN keeper remains) | 100 pF | 4.2 µs | Entirely inadequate |

**Conclusion:** 60 cm + 5 devices is a relatively long I3C topology. The STM32 internal pull-up is **theoretically sufficient but marginal**; SCL/SDA rising edges must be checked with an oscilloscope during bring-up.

## 5. Why “one device worked before, but multiple devices failed”

**Single-device success was fortunate marginal operation, not genuine stability.** The mechanism:

1. **The STM32's ordinary GPIO default weak pull-up of ~40 kΩ** was unknowingly helping to pull the bus High. Note: this is **not** the dedicated PWR_I3CPUCR pull-up; it is the default weak GPIO pull-up, an order of magnitude weaker.
2. **The I3C startup handshake uses OD mode**, which depends on pull-ups. This phase was marginal but happened to pass.
3. **After the handshake, the bus enters the push-pull SDR data phase**, in which the MCU actively drives both levels. Rise time is no longer RC-limited, **masking the marginal OD phase**.

### Calculation

| Scenario | Bus C | RPU (ordinary GPIO 40 kΩ default) | t_rise | Assessment against specification |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 device + < 10 cm jumper wires** | 25 pF | 40 kΩ | 847 ns | Barely within I²C Standard's 1000 ns; **far outside I3C limits** |
| **5 devices + 60 cm** | 145 pF | 40 kΩ | 4.9 µs | 5 times the Standard limit; **I3C impossible** |

**Individual capacitance references:**

- Per pin (SDA or SCL) on an I²C/I3C device: specification maximum 10 pF; measured sensor values 5–10 pF.
- Dupont / jumper wires: ~1 pF/cm.
- PCB trace over a ground plane: 1–2 pF/cm.
- STM32 pin + package: approximately 5 pF.

**Conclusion:** any setup beyond a single board and single sensor must have dedicated pull-ups (STM32U385 PWR_I3CPUCR ~2 kΩ, or external 1–2.2 kΩ).

---

## 6. Final decision for the SpineSense expansion board

**β decision (locked on the evening of 5-17):** fit SDA/SCL 0402 SMD pull-up resistors on the expansion board, connected to VDDIO = 3.3V, with resistance **1–2.2 kΩ** (the designer selects the exact value based on bus length + signal integrity).

**Reasons:**

- The STM32U385 PWR_I3CPUCR internal pull-up can theoretically replace external resistors, but 60 cm + 5 devices is a marginal topology (see §4.5: estimated 170–246 ns, close to / slightly beyond the I3C OD specification).
- On-board resistors are physically close to the bus endpoint, giving more stable rise times.
- Factory fitting saves a measurement step compared with leaving footprints for later soldering.
- If the internal pull-up is also enabled, the two pull-ups act in parallel → lower equivalent resistance → more stable rise times.

**Firmware must still enable the internal pull-ups**, because PWR_I3CPUCR is officially recommended by ST and also serves as redundancy.
